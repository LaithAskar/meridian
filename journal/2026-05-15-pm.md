## Explanation

### Why the S&P 100 (OEX) as a proxy

The task asks for "top-100 most liquid US equities by Jan 2016 market cap." The S&P 100 (ticker: OEX) is exactly that concept institutionalized: S&P selects the 100 largest S&P 500 components with active listed options. It is published daily, audited, and well-known enough to be interview-defensible without needing to justify a methodology from scratch. The alternative — pulling point-in-time market cap data from a paid database — introduces an unnecessary dependency for what the DESIGN.md already acknowledges as "second-best survivorship-bias defense."

### Ticker substitutions for Alpaca continuity

Two companies changed their ticker symbols mid-window without any economic discontinuity (same equity, same cash flows, same CUSIP). Storing the 2016 ticker would produce a gap in Alpaca data:

- **FB → META** (Oct 2022): Facebook renamed itself Meta Platforms and changed its Nasdaq ticker. Alpaca serves the full IPO-to-present history under `META`.
- **PCLN → BKNG** (May 2018): Priceline Group renamed itself Booking Holdings. Alpaca serves the full history under `BKNG`.

Using the current ticker is the correct choice for the bulk-download script (task 0.3): querying `META` returns bars from 2012–2024 without a gap; querying `FB` against Alpaca would likely return nothing (the old ticker is retired).

### Partial-history tickers kept in the list

Eight companies were acquired or merged during the 2016–2024 window. Their bars simply stop at the event date. The engine (task 1.3) treats missing bars as "no position" — zero weight, no fill, no P&L impact. These stubs are worth keeping in the universe because:

1. They represent real contemporaneous investment decisions a manager running this strategy would have faced.
2. Dropping them retroactively would be a subtle form of survivorship bias (removing names that "didn't make it through").
3. Their weight is small in an equal-weight 100-stock universe (≤1% per name), so the impact of gaps is bounded.

The worst case is EMC, acquired in September 2016 — barely nine months of live data. It is in the list because it was a legitimate S&P 100 component in January 2016.

### DD and DOW: the DowDuPont gap

This is the most complicated case. Dow Chemical (DOW) and DuPont (DD) merged into DowDuPont (DWDP) in September 2017, then the combined entity split back into three separate companies in 2019 — Dow Inc. (DOW), DuPont de Nemours (DD), and Corteva (CTVA). The current `DOW` and `DD` on Alpaca are the 2019 relistings, not the pre-merger originals.

Effect in practice: bars for DOW and DD will be available 2016-01 through ~2017-09, absent through 2019, then present again as a different economic entity. The engine will simply hold no position during the gap year. Given that each name represents 1% of the portfolio, this has minimal impact on aggregate metrics. A more rigorous approach would substitute DWDP during 2017–2019 and then map to DOW+DD, but that complexity is disproportionate to the benefit in a 100-stock universe.

### BRK.B ticker format

`"BRK.B".upper() == "BRK.B"` — the period passes the uppercase assertion because Python's `.upper()` leaves non-alpha characters unchanged. The Alpaca API accepts the `BRK.B` format (NYSE standard). This is noted in the module docstring.

### Alternatives rejected

- **Pulling the list at runtime from a reference dataset**: introduces a dependency (Wikipedia scraping, paid data API) and runtime fragility. A hardcoded list is the right call for a held-constant universe.
- **Using yfinance's `tickers_sp500()` snapshot**: returns the *current* S&P 500, not the 2016 list. Significant survivorship bias risk.
- **Using a smaller universe (50 stocks)**: the DESIGN.md specifies 100. No reason to deviate.
- **Excluding all partial-history tickers**: see "Partial-history tickers" above — would introduce retroactive survivorship bias.
